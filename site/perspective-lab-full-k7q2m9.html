Perspective Lab

Perspective is not a trick of rulers. It is simply where you are standing. In this lab you will find the eye level and vanishing points inside one of my paintings, then move them yourself and watch the whole scene change.

"Before you draw a building, decide where your eyes are. Everything else follows from that."

Part 1

Find the perspective in a real painting

Turn on the guides one at a time. Look at how the lines of the roof and the base of the building lean toward the same points.

Vaughn Tucker, Down Town Kingstown, 22 × 28 in, 2021. Approximate analysis of the painting. Each colored line family shares one vanishing point; expressive painted edges may differ from this idealized construction.

This is two-point perspective. We are looking at the corner of the building, so each wall runs away from us toward its own vanishing point.

The eye level is low, about the height of a person standing on the street. That is why we look up at the roof and see the underside of the balcony, but we see the top of the curb.

Both vanishing points are outside the canvas. When they are far apart, the building looks calm and natural. If they were close together, the corner would look sharp and stretched.

Part 2

Now move it yourself

Drag the eye level line up and down. Drag the vanishing points (the colored circles). You can also drag the boxes around. Read the notes as you go.

Tip: on a phone or iPad, drag with one finger.

Notes from Vaughn

    Part 3

    Try it on paper

    You need a pencil, a ruler and a sheet of paper. Draw lightly at first; these are construction lines.

    1. Three boxes, one eye level

    Draw a horizon line across your page and one vanishing point. Draw one box below the line, one crossing it and one floating above it. Which boxes show their top? Which show their bottom?

    2. The street corner

    Draw a low horizon line and two vanishing points near the edges of the page. Draw one tall vertical line for the corner of a building, then connect its top and bottom to both vanishing points.

    3. Look around you

    Stand at a window or on a street. Hold your pencil flat at eye level. Find parallel horizontal edges receding into the distance: above eye level they slope down toward their vanishing point; below eye level they slope up. Upright edges and sloping roofs follow different rules.
